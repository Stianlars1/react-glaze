A smoother glass edge.

The stepped refraction came from the new rounded geometry. Small faces along the bevel bent light in different directions. The original Drawn To lens already had smooth surface normals.

The fix lets adjacent faces share those normals. The images below use the same background, size, resolution and material settings. Only the geometry normals changed. No extra blur was added.

Browser captures: Chrome, 1536 × 1024 viewport, 660 × 280 CSS-pixel glass surface, actual DPR 1. These crops were captured directly in the browser. Zoom also enlarges the screenshot pixels.

Hold, drag and explore.

Drag the glass with a mouse or touch, use Alt + arrow keys, or select Center. Dragging does not trigger the button’s click action. Drag behavior belongs to the playground; the reusable component accepts normal HTML props.

Explore three original wallpapers, Shuffle, material controls, sharp or refracted content, and JSX/JSON export. The default keeps the reference’s pronounced refraction.

What has been checked?

Controls and background changes were checked in Chrome 152, the Firefox 155 test build, and the WebKit 26.5 test engine. Checks covered mouse input, keyboard input, touch emulation, glass on/off, mobile layout, animated backgrounds and pausing. A separate Next.js 16.3.4 app built and used the packed React component, including two simultaneous surfaces and unmounting.

Testing Safari 26.4 on Mac also exposed missing embedded images in SVG snapshots. The WebKit path now waits 120 ms before redrawing a snapshot. This fixed the observed background changes, but slows continuous background capture. Dragging sharp glass over an unchanged background reuses the snapshot.

This is an alpha preview. Arbitrary DOM, video, cross-origin iframes, complex CSS and physical iPhone/Android devices still need further validation. Low DPR, especially 0.5, can still produce ordinary pixelation. See the research notes for the current limits.
